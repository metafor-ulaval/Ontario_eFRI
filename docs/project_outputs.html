1 Automated stand delineation

In this section and the next one, the “SPL + S2 red edge” segmentation model was use. As a reminder, the metrics used for this model are theses one : z_p95 + z_above2 + B6. There is a visual example of the metrics used in the section 2.4 of Data and Methods. For both the Romeo Malette Forest (RMF) and Ottawa Valley Forest (OVF), distribution of area and shape index will be showed for the whole Forest Management Unit scale (in yellow in the figure 1 and 2). Furthermore, a visual example of resulting automated stand delineation that was smoothed in ArcGIS will be shown for a closer subset area (in red in the figure 1 and 2). The restricted area of 5x5 km allows for easier visualization.

The resulting polygons can be expected to represent homogeneous forest stands based on choosen metrics. The automated stand delineations can be now compared to photo-interpreted forest inventory polygons for the whole Forest Management Units. Density plots are used to assess whether the distribution of the size and shape are similar. Based on previous parameters obtained by the segmentation parameters optimisation, the difference between distributions are supposed to be minimal and therefore the automated stand delineation should represent operationally plausible forest stand that mimic polygons of the photo-interpreted forest inventory polygons.

Area in hectare is use for size and shape index is use to assess polygon shape. The shape index is determine by this formula :

\[ ShapeIndex = \frac{\text{Perimeter}}{\sqrt{\pi \times \text{Area}}} \]

The density plot were computed at the pixel level and are therefore area-weighted.

1.2 Additionnal parameters

It is recommended to perform a smoothing step that can be made later in ArcGIS Pro or any GIS with a smoothing tool that keep the topology of adjacent polygons. The tool use in this case is “Smooth Polygon” of the toolbox “Cartography”. The parameters used are as follow :

  • algorithm = “PAEK”;
  • tolerance = 100;
  • endpoint_option = “FIXED_ENDPOINT”
  • error_option = “RESOLVE_ERRORS”.

The next figure shows automated stand delineation before smoothing.

With smoothing, the polygons looks more natural and the border seems to better mimic the edges of actual forest stands.

An additional data that can be used in automated stand delineation are masks where there is no forest and therefore the segmentation is not performed. These are typically waterbodies and roads.

Here is the resulting automated stand delineation when the masks are considered. It is important to note that the Generic Region Merging algorithm is extremely sensitive to any parameters change. Every slightly input change will drastically change the resulting polygons, even a slightly bigger extent where the algorithm is run. This is the reason why the polygons in the figure x are different from the one at the figure x, even if parameters and metrics as input are the same.

3 Performance of automated stand delineations

3.1 Visual comparison of automated stand delineations

Polygon boundaries are overlaid on the high-resolution forest-attribute layers used for performance assessment, allowing visual comparison of how the different delineations partition spatial variation in canopy height, merchantable volume, and stem density.

Figure 24 shows a closed-canopy area where structural transitions are mostly gradual. The delineations based on SPL metrics (“SPL only” and “SPL + S2 red edge”) closely follow the patch of tall, high-volume forest in the north-east and separate it from the lower and less stocked stands in the south. The photo-interpreted polygons and the “S2 only” delineation capture the same general pattern, but several of their boundaries cross gradients of canopy height and merchantable volume rather than following them. Stem density shows little contrast in this area (approximately 1500 to 1900 stems/ha), so none of the delineations is strongly structured by this attribute.

Figure 25 shows a contrasted landscape where open, low vegetation areas alternate with tall, dense stands. In this situation, the main transitions are captured by all delineations, including the photo-interpreted map. The delineations based on SPL metrics further subdivide the forested areas according to differences in canopy height and merchantable volume, for example by isolating the high-volume stand on the west side from the lower stands surrounding it. The “S2 only” delineation mainly follows the spectral contrast between open and forested areas and tends to group stands that differ in structure into larger polygons.

3.2 Partitioning of forest-attribute variability

The proportion of variance explained (PVE) was used to assess how effectively each delineation partitioned spatial variability in the reference forest attributes. Higher PVE indicates that a greater proportion of the landscape-level variability is represented by differences among polygons.

As shown in Figure 26, the two delineations based on SPL metrics explained more of the variability of the reference forest attributes than the photo-interpreted map in nearly all cases. Gains were larger in OVF, ranging from +3.5 to +9.2 percentage points, with the largest improvements for canopy height and QMDBH. In RMF, the “SPL only” delineation provided consistent gains (+2.3 to +6.8 percentage points), while the “SPL + S2 red edge” delineation performed similarly to the photo-interpreted map (-2.2 to +1.2 percentage points). In contrast, the “S2 only” delineation systematically explained less variability than the photo-interpreted map, with losses of 9.6 to 26.8 percentage points. Spectral information alone is therefore not sufficient to partition forest structure, whereas structural information from SPL is the main driver of the gains observed.

3.3 Internal polygon homogeneity

Internal polygon homogeneity was assessed using the area-weighted mean within-polygon standard deviation (wSD) of the reference forest attributes. Lower values indicate greater structural homogeneity within delineated polygons.

The results for internal homogeneity (Figure 27) closely mirror those obtained for PVE. In OVF, both delineations based on SPL metrics produced polygons that were more homogeneous than the photo-interpreted ones for all attributes, with reductions of wSD between 4.6 % and 8.6 %. In RMF, the “SPL only” delineation also improved homogeneity for all attributes (+3.0 % to +8.6 %), the largest gain being for merchantable volume, while the “SPL + S2 red edge” delineation remained close to the photo-interpreted map (-1.4 % to +1.8 %). The “S2 only” delineation produced markedly more variable polygons in both Forest Management Units (-11.3 % to -27.4 %).

3.4 Correspondence of stand boundaries with structural transitions

The spatial correspondence between mapped stand boundaries and independently detected structural transitions was evaluated using precision, recall, and F1-score. This analysis assesses the extent to which delineated boundaries coincide with changes in forest structure identified directly from the high-resolution reference layers.

The correspondence between stand boundaries and structural transitions (Figure 28) shows the largest differences between Forest Management Units. In OVF, the delineations based on SPL metrics strongly improved the correspondence with detected transitions, particularly for canopy height (+29.5 % and +34.1 %) and QMDBH (+27.9 % and +35.9 %). The “SPL + S2 red edge” delineation performed best for three of the four attributes. In RMF, improvements were limited: the “SPL only” delineation improved correspondence for merchantable volume (+12.8 %) but was close to the photo-interpreted map for the other attributes, and the “SPL + S2 red edge” delineation showed slightly poorer correspondence for merchantable volume (-5.9 %) and stem density (-8.6 %). The “S2 only” delineation had poorer correspondence than the photo-interpreted map in both Forest Management Units, with the largest losses in OVF (-18.6 % to -30.1 %).

Overall, the three criteria lead to consistent conclusions. Automated stand delineations based on SPL metrics performed as well as or better than the photo-interpreted forest inventory map, with clear gains in OVF and more modest gains in RMF. The “SPL only” model was the most consistent across Forest Management Units, while adding the Sentinel-2 red edge band improved boundary correspondence in OVF but not in RMF. Delineations based only on Sentinel-2 imagery performed systematically worse than the photo-interpreted map and are not recommended for structural stand delineation.
